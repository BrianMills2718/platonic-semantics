Three language models organise politics the same way as each other — and not the way people do

Ten political concepts, positioned by how they are actually used. Four corpora, one identical instrument, matched to the same amount of text: 3,430 posts written by people, and posts written by 3 models built by different organisations (openai, deepseek, zhipu), each asked to post about topics drawn from the human corpus and never shown a human post.

Every pair, against the ceiling

Brighter means closer agreement. The diagonal is each corpus against itself — the most any comparison could show. The three models form a bright block; people stay dark against all of them.

Where the difference lives

One block per concept. The other nine sit on its axis at their measured distance from it, one lane per corpus, joined by a thread. Where a thread runs straight between the model lanes and then kinks at the people lane, the models agree and people differ — those threads are drawn in amber. Nothing is projected: every position drawn is a number that was measured.

Distances sit in a narrow band, normal for this measure, so the axis is stretched to the observed range rather than 0–1; the ordering of neighbours carries the meaning. Ceilings are split-half correlations corrected to full length by Spearman–Brown — uncorrected, they are measured on half the text the comparisons use and understate badly.

No 2-D map appears here on purpose: two dimensions retain only about 31% of this structure, so most of what such a map showed would be an artefact of the flattening rather than a measurement.
