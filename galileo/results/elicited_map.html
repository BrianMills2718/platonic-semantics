The elicited space — three models, one frame, nothing aligned

Ten political concepts, positioned from distances the models were asked for: each judged every pair against one shared rod ("good" and "evil" are 100 apart), over 25 randomised orders, twice. Because they share the rod their numbers are already in the same units — so all three sit in one frame with no alignment step. That commensurability is what Woelfel's method exists to provide, and it is exactly what the text-counting route gave up.

This is drawn in three dimensions, and rotates, because two would not have been honest. Flattened to a plane these distances keep only 54% of their structure and the picture visibly fails — president, vote, party and biden collapse into one blob, and the worst pair is drawn at 0.11 having been elicited at 0.95. Three dimensions keep 68%, which clears the bar this project set for drawing anything spatially. The bar was not moved to permit a picture; the picture gained a dimension to meet it.

Each model agrees with itself at 0.97 and with the others at about 0.84, so spread between the coloured dots is real disagreement, not noise.

What a model states here has essentially nothing to do with what its own writing implies — the same concepts measured by word co-occurrence give ρ = 0.01 against these. See ASKED_VS_COUNTED.md.
